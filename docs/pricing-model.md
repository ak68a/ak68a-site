# Pricing Model

**For:** Internal reference — what I charge, why, and how it maps to each service line.

---

## Market Context

Fractional CTOs typically charge $150–$500/hr or $4,000–$20,000+/mo retainer. That range is wide because "fractional CTO" covers everything from a monthly Zoom call to near full-time embedded leadership.

The market segments into three tiers:

| Tier | Monthly | Hours/Week | Typical Scope |
|------|---------|------------|---------------|
| Advisory | $3,000–$6,000 | 2–5 | Strategic check-ins, architectural reviews |
| Standard | $6,000–$15,000 | 10–20 | Active technical leadership, team management |
| Intensive | $15,000–$25,000+ | 25–40 | Near full-time, MVP builds, major transitions |

---

## Why I Price Above Median

Most fractional CTOs are advisors. They review, they recommend, they leave. I architect AND build. That collapses two roles (CTO + lead engineer) into one billing relationship, which is cheaper for the client even at a higher hourly rate.

**Multipliers that justify premium pricing:**

1. **Architecture + execution in one person.** Clients don't need to hire a separate architect or senior engineer to translate my recommendations into working systems. The architecture docs aren't theoretical — they're tested against real provider APIs and real code.

2. **AI-native development methodology.** My agent pipeline (planner, implementer, reviewer with structural security enforcement) means I produce at 3–4x the pace of a traditional senior engineer. Clients get more output per dollar spent.

3. **Fintech domain depth.** 65+ provider APIs researched across 9 categories. I don't need ramp-up time on payments infrastructure, compliance requirements, or provider selection. Day one is productive.

4. **Compliance built in, not bolted on.** SOC 2 readiness, OFAC screening, NACHA rules — designed into the architecture from the start. This avoids the expensive rework that happens when teams build first and audit later.

## Service Pricing

### 1. Fractional CTO

The lead service. Everything else ladders up to this. Two modes depending on what the client actually needs.

#### Mode A: CTO + Builder (Architecture & Engineering)

For clients who need technical leadership AND hands-on execution. I'm directing the team AND writing code / designing systems.

| Engagement | Rate | Scope |
|------------|------|-------|
| Advisory | $5,000/mo (≈$310/hr at 4 hrs/wk) | Weekly strategy calls, architecture reviews, hiring input, board-level technical guidance |
| Standard | $12,000/mo (≈$300/hr at 10 hrs/wk) | Active technical leadership, vendor evaluation, code reviews, team management, sprint cadence |
| Intensive | $20,000/mo (≈$250/hr at 20 hrs/wk) | Near full-time leadership, hands-on architecture + building, critical transitions |

**Why these numbers:** Advisory is priced at the high end of market ($5K vs $3–6K range) because even limited hours deliver fintech domain depth + AI methodology that most advisors can't. Standard and Intensive rates come DOWN per hour as commitment goes up — the client is buying consistency, I'm getting predictable revenue.

#### Mode B: Pure CTO (Leadership Only)

For clients who already have engineers but lack technical leadership. I'm not building — I'm steering. Hiring, firing decisions, team structure, engineering culture, vendor strategy, board reporting, technical due diligence, roadmap ownership.

| Engagement | Rate | Scope |
|------------|------|-------|
| Advisory | $4,000/mo (≈$250/hr at 4 hrs/wk) | Bi-weekly or weekly strategy calls, architecture direction, hiring pipeline review |
| Standard | $8,000/mo (≈$200/hr at 10 hrs/wk) | Weekly cadence with the team, sprint oversight, 1:1s with leads, vendor decisions, board prep |
| Intensive | $14,000/mo (≈$175/hr at 20 hrs/wk) | Acting CTO — standups, hiring loops, performance management, investor/board interface, roadmap ownership |

**Why Mode B is priced lower per-hour:** The value prop is different. Mode A collapses CTO + lead engineer into one person — that's rare and commands a premium. Mode B is leadership and judgment, which is valuable but not as scarce. The client still gets fintech domain depth, AI methodology guidance for their team, and a CTO who actually understands the code — but I'm not writing it.

**Why Mode B is still above market:** Most fractional CTOs in "advisory" mode are generalists. I bring fintech-specific domain depth (payments, compliance, provider landscape), AI engineering methodology (I can train their team on agent-assisted workflows), and I've actually built the systems I'm advising on. That operational credibility is worth the premium over a pure strategist.

**When to recommend Mode A vs Mode B:**
- Mode A: Early-stage startup (pre-Series A), no engineering team yet, or team is junior and needs a technical ceiling
- Mode B: Team is competent but directionless, founder is non-technical and needs a technical co-pilot, company is scaling and needs someone to own engineering culture/hiring/process
- Either: Post-acquisition integration, major platform migration, compliance overhaul

**Minimum engagement:** 3 months. Anything shorter is a project, not a CTO relationship.

## Pricing Principles

1. **Never compete on hourly rate.** Compete on output per dollar. $250/hr that ships in a week beats $150/hr that ships in a month.

2. **Retainers over hourly where possible.** Predictable revenue, deeper client relationships, better outcomes because I have full context.

3. **Bundle when it makes sense.** A Fractional CTO client who also needs security work shouldn't be paying standalone security rates. The CTO retainer covers it.

4. **Project pricing for discrete deliverables.** Architecture designs, due diligence reports, AI workflow setups — these have clear start/end points and deliverables. Price the outcome, not the hours.

5. **Premium is justified by speed + depth.** The combination of fintech domain expertise, AI-native methodology, and architecture-to-execution capability is rare. Price accordingly.
